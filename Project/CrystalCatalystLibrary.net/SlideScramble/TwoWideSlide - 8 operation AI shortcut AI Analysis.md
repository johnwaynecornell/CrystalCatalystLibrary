## Why this is shorter

The opportunity is to move pairs of tiles in the two lower rows together, while using row 4 rotations to change their relative alignment. The two paired swaps bring row 3 to identity; the final row 4 rotation completes row 4. The solution uses the same paired-exchange/parity reasoning as the earlier Portable Lens analysis, but leverages alignment to avoid several intermediate corrections.

I used a breadth-first search starting at the lower-half state after operation 4. The search included all three nonzero rotations of either lower row and adjacent paired ColumnSwaps across both lower rows (including wraparound). It found a shortest remaining distance of **four logical operations**; no continuation of three or fewer exists **while keeping rows 1 and 2 untouched and using these lower-half operations**. This does **not** establish a globally shortest path from the original scrambled board, since a different opening could be shorter.
